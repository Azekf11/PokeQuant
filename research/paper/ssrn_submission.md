## Title

Opening or Buying? A Probabilistic and Risk-Adjusted Comparison of Pokémon TCG Booster Acquisition and Secondary-Market Purchases

## Author

Anh-Vu TA, Independent Researcher

## Date written

November 2026 (version 3)

## Abstract (1,202 characters)

Collectors of Pokémon trading cards can obtain a card by opening booster packs or by buying it on the secondary market. I compare the two strategies for three recent English sets with a slot-level model of the booster pack, Bayesian posteriors on community-measured pull rates, and a nested Monte Carlo simulation (200 posterior draws × 20,000 runs). Cards are valued at dated TCGplayer market prices net of published fees, shipping and a liquidity haircut. Hypotheses, metrics and decision rules were pre-registered. Under central costs the break-even booster price is 1.03 to 1.42 USD, against a retail price of 4.49 USD. Opening 36 boosters loses 111 to 125 USD on average, and buying the same value directly is better on the mean, the median and the 5 % conditional value-at-risk in 35 of 36 comparisons. The average loss from opening holds in sixteen robustness variants, on eleven further sets and at European sale prices, and buying stays decisively better for every budget of ten boosters or more in all fourteen sets. Selling costs drive the result: for two sets, a pack's gross market value exceeds its retail price. Pull-rate uncertainty widens every interval but changes no main conclusion.
