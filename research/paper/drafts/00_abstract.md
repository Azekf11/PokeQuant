# Abstract

Collectors of Pokémon trading cards can get cards by opening booster packs or
by buying them on the secondary market. I compare the two strategies for three
recent English sets with a slot-level model of the booster pack, Bayesian
posteriors on community-measured pull rates and a nested Monte Carlo
simulation (200 posterior draws × 20,000 runs). Cards are valued at dated
TCGplayer market prices, net of published fees, shipping and a liquidity
haircut. Hypotheses, metrics and decision rules were pre-registered. Under
central costs a booster breaks even at 1.03 to 1.42 USD; it retails at 4.49
USD. Opening 36 boosters loses 111 to 125 USD on average. Buying cards
directly with the same budget also loses money through round-trip costs, but
less than half as much. Across sets and budgets, buying is decisively better
on the mean, the median and the 5 % conditional value-at-risk in 35 of 36
comparisons. The average loss from opening holds in sixteen robustness
variants, on eleven further sets and at European sale prices, and buying stays
decisively better for every budget of ten boosters or more in all fourteen
sets. Selling costs drive the result: in two sets a pack's gross market value
is above its retail price, and selling costs alone turn that margin into a
loss. Pull-rate uncertainty widens every interval but changes no main
conclusion.

*Keywords:* collectibles, loot boxes, Monte Carlo simulation, Bayesian
uncertainty, expected shortfall, pre-registration.
